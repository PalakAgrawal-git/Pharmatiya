Phase 1 · Website rebuild

Pharmatiya Health: what we are building

A new six-page website, replacing pharmatiya.net — and what we need from you to deliver it.

Prepared by Vaelo Creative Date 3 September 2026 Launch 23 October 2026

In short

We are rebuilding pharmatiya.net from scratch as a six-page website, built to convince the people who actually buy HEOR and real-world evidence work.

The current site does not show what Pharmatiya is capable of. It reads like a template — because it is one. Twenty-five years of specialist experience sits behind generic wording, and the site has no working way for a prospective client to get in touch.

The new site fixes both problems: it makes the expertise visible, and it makes it possible to act on.

What we found

We went through the current site page by page. These are the issues that matter commercially.

There is no way to contact you
Three pages invite visitors to get in touch, but there is no form anywhere on the site. The email address in the footer is not clickable either.
A working contact form, sorted by enquiry type, plus a clickable email address and direct consultation booking.
“Schedule your appointment” is not a button
It looks like a call to action but nothing happens when you click it. There is no way to book anything.
A real booking route on every page, connected to your calendar.
Your Services page is hidden
It exists, but it is missing from the main menu. Visitors can only reach it through the homepage.
Services in the main navigation, with each service reachable directly.
Unfinished template images are live
Five images on the site are still the placeholders that came with the template, including on the homepage.
Real photography and genuine research graphics in every one of those positions.
There is a shopping cart
A leftover from the template. It sits on every page and suggests you sell products online.
Removed completely.
Your best material is buried
The About page holds everything that proves your expertise — the datasets, the methods, the Outcomes Research division you built — in long paragraphs nobody reads to the end.
Broken into sections a busy buyer can scan in ten seconds, with the depth still there underneath.
Your AI tool is almost invisible
It appears as a single menu link pointing off-site. Nothing explains what it does or why it can be trusted.
Its own page, explaining the workflow, the human review process and how patient data is handled.
Nothing on the site is specific
Phrases like “tailored solutions” and “actionable insights” repeat across pages. Any consultancy in any industry could have written them.
Real numbers, named datasets, named methods and concrete deliverables.

The six pages

Home Says clearly what Pharmatiya does and who for, with proof visible immediately — 25+ years, study volume, therapeutic areas — rather than buried. One clear action: book a consultation.
Services Your three service lines, each framed around a problem your buyer recognises, followed by your approach, your methods and exactly what they receive at the end.
Evidence A new page, and the most important one. It answers the question every serious buyer asks: why should we believe you can do this? Case studies, dataset coverage, therapeutic areas and methods. Needs case study material from you
NextGen AI A new page for your AI tool. Explains the three-step workflow, where AI is used and where your researchers remain in control, and how patient data is protected. Needs product detail from you
About Twenty-five years made scannable: track record, milestones, the team with real photographs and credentials, and your dataset experience. Needs team photography from you
Contact Separate routes for new clients, existing clients and press, so enquiries reach the right person. Plus consultation booking and a working signup for the Quarterly Insights Digest.

How it will look

Restrained and precise — closer to a research publication than a technology startup. The visual character comes from your own field: survival curves, forest plots and cohort diagrams used as design elements, in the places a template would put stock photography.

We will not use stock photos of scientists in laboratories, rows of identical feature boxes, or gradient banners. Those are the visual habits that make a company look like everyone else, and they would undercut exactly the expertise we are trying to communicate.

The site will work properly on phones, meet international accessibility standards, and load quickly.

What we need from you

We cannot build a credible site without real material. Everything below is grouped by when we need it.

By Friday 11 September

Three decisions at kickoff

  • Dataset names. Can we name Aetna/CVS, Cigna, Intermountain and the others publicly? A simple yes or no for each.
  • Where enquiries should go. Who receives new business, existing client questions and press? And a calendar link if you want direct booking.
  • Compliance turnaround. How long does an internal review take? We need the timing, not the review itself, so we can schedule around it.

By Friday 18 September

Before design begins

  • Photographs of the team, the office and people working. No stock imagery will be substituted.
  • Your logo as a vector file (SVG or AI). The current one is a low-resolution image.
  • Typeface approval. We will suggest options; budget around $200–500 one-time.

By Friday 2 October

Before the pages are built

  • Two or three case studies, anonymised. This is the strongest proof of expertise you can give us.
  • Service descriptions checked. We write the drafts; you confirm they are accurate. Nothing is published without your sign-off.
  • NextGen AI details: screenshots, how the workflow runs, what it does and does not do.

By Friday 9 October

Before launch

  • Hosting preference. The new site is not tied to Squarespace and can be hosted anywhere. We are happy to recommend an option.

Two quick answers we need now: should the AI tool be called NextGen AI or RWE - Builder publicly? And can you confirm the “1,000+ synopses” figure? We would rather leave a number off the homepage than publish one we cannot stand behind.
